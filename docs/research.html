Research

How can intelligent systems become more reliable, understandable, and useful to the people who depend on them?

My primary focus is on language and vision–language systems and agentic AI: how models reason over evidence, how agents coordinate, and how failures can be detected and understood.

I work across related areas in human–AI interaction, explainability, sensing, and Edge AI. These connections inform the questions I ask; they represent different stages of experience and ongoing learning, rather than equal claims of specialization.

LLMs & agentic AI

How can language models and specialized agents share evidence and check one another without amplifying errors?

Questions I am exploring

  • How does unreliable intermediate information propagate across an agent workflow?
  • When does cross-agent verification improve the reliability of a final decision?

Current investigations

These are ongoing projects described in my latest CVs. They are distinct from completed, peer-reviewed work; no final results are claimed here.

Work in progress

Multimodal agentic AI systems

Developing a LangGraph-based framework in which specialized agents process multimodal evidence, exchange context, coordinate reasoning, and perform cross-agent verification. The study examines information sharing, intermediate failures, and error propagation.

Work in progress

Trust calibration in LLMs and VLMs

Investigating how model confidence, uncertainty, explanations, and high-confidence errors shape appropriate human reliance on language and multimodal AI.

Work in progress

Multilingual prompting and tokenization

Studying Bangla, Banglish, and English prompting across models to examine response quality, hallucination, confidence, and reliability.

Work in progress

Reliable Edge AI under distribution shift

Investigating robust evaluation and uncertainty-aware adaptation as sensing conditions, deployment environments, and input distributions change.

Work in progress

Neuroadaptive AI from 32-channel EEG

Co-investigator on a MIST research grant led by Dr. T. M. Shahriar Sazzad, developing a self-supervised Transformer pipeline for cognitive workload, vigilance, and affective-state modeling with interpretable closed-loop feedback.

Work in progress

Explainable skin-lesion classification

Developing a DINO-ViT framework on HAM10000 with uncertainty quantification, visual explanations, fairness analysis, and clinician-centered evaluation.

Work in progress

Network decision support and adaptive estimation

Studying AI-assisted decisions in simulated 5G networks and learning-assisted state estimation under time-varying sensing noise. These are ongoing investigations, with no completed findings claimed here.
